How DecisionRAG works

Find the right passage by walking the corpus with a decision model (screen documents, score passages, optionally rerank), not by searching an index. Pipeline from Figure 2(c) of arXiv:2610.35868.
Input

Question

The user asks, for example, "How do Kafka clients authenticate to Event Hubs?"

query q
Before the query

The corpus is a tree

Topics, documents, sections, passages: the structure it already has. No embeddings, no index to build or refresh.

structure, not an index
Stage 1

Document screening

One yes/no question per document, all judged together in one shared context. Keep the top k. Optional categories can narrow first.

1 call, keep top k
Stage 2

Passage scoring

Yes/no per passage, only for the kept documents. Pruned documents are never expanded or scored. Keep the top r.

1 call, keep top r
Stage 3, optional

Choice rerank

One multiple-choice question over the r passages ("which best answers it?") gives the final order.

1 call, over r passages
Output

Answer

The generator answers from the passages and cites topic > document > section (and page for PDFs). Weak evidence: it abstains.

grounded + cited

Why it is fast

One model call per stage, never one per passage: two to three calls (plus one for the optional rerank) however big the corpus is. A step with a single option needs no call.

Why "together" matters

All candidates of a stage share one context, so the model weighs them against each other and the scores can be ranked. Independent scoring (an ablation switch) can drift between candidates.

Safety

If the best passage is improbable it abstains; if the decision model fails or answers badly it abstains with a reason instead of guessing. Retrieved text is untrusted data.

What it needs

A corpus with real structure (titles, headings). No embedding index, no first-stage retriever, no task-specific training. A flat pile of unlabelled text is a poor fit.

Honest notes. The pipeline follows the paper's Figure 2 and abstract; the paper's default k and r, prompts and tree construction were not available, so the defaults here are this example's choices. The numbers in the worked example come from the offline word-matching stand-in, not from Jev or Claude, and say nothing about the paper's accuracy results.
